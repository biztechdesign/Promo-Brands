How to Choose the Right Branded Merchandise for Your Next Event

Whether you’re planning a trade show, conference, or corporate away-day, the branded merchandise you choose says everything about your company. Get it right and your brand lives on long after the event ends. Get it wrong and it ends up in the bin before delegates reach the car park.

Promotional products remain one of the most cost-effective marketing channels available to UK businesses. According to the BPMA, 79% of people can recall the branding on a promotional product they received in the past two years — a recall rate that outperforms most digital advertising. But volume alone doesn’t guarantee impact. The product, the print quality, and the relevance to the recipient are what separate memorable merchandise from landfill fodder.

1. Start With Your Audience, Not Your Budget

The most common mistake event managers make is opening with a budget conversation before understanding who will receive the product. A tech startup audience at a product launch will respond very differently to the same item than delegates at a financial services conference. Ask yourself:

  • Who is the primary recipient — clients, prospects, employees, or press?
  • What is the occasion — a reward, a welcome pack, a leave-behind, or an activation?
  • What do they already carry, use, or value?
  • Will they be travelling to the event, or based locally?

A reusable coffee cup is a thoughtful gift for a sustainability-focused audience. The same cup given at a drinks industry trade show may land poorly if wine is the product of the day. Context is everything.

2. Choose Products People Will Actually Use

Usability determines longevity. Every time a recipient uses your branded item, you receive a free brand impression. The most effective promotional products tend to fall into a handful of high-use categories:

  • Drinkware — reusable bottles, travel mugs, and tumblers generate thousands of impressions over their lifespan
  • Bags — tote bags, backpacks, and shoppers are carried publicly, multiplying your reach
  • Notebooks & stationery — used daily at desks and in meetings, keeping your brand in constant view
  • Tech accessories — cables, power banks, and earbuds are valued and rarely discarded
  • Clothing — branded hoodies and polo shirts extend brand reach beyond the event itself

“The best promotional product is the one that sits on a recipient’s desk, not in their recycling bin. Utility wins every time.”

— James Fielding, Head of Sourcing, Promo Brand

3. Match Print Method to Product and Purpose

Print quality is the detail that separates professional merchandise from cheap giveaways. The decoration method you choose affects durability, colour accuracy, and cost. Here’s a quick guide:

  1. Screen printing — ideal for flat surfaces with 1–4 solid colours; excellent for bags, t-shirts, and notebooks
  2. Embroidery — premium finish for clothing and bags; adds texture and longevity but suits simpler logos
  3. Digital transfer (DTF/DTG) — full-colour detail on fabric; best for complex artwork or photographic prints
  4. Laser engraving — sophisticated and permanent on metal, wood, and glass; ideal for gifts and awards
  5. Pad printing — precise single or multi-colour print on small or irregular surfaces like pens and USB drives

Our team will always recommend the most suitable method for your artwork and product combination. If in doubt, request a pre-production sample before committing to full volume.

4. Plan Lead Times Early — Very Early

Nothing derails a merchandise programme faster than underestimating lead times. Standard branded products typically require 10–15 working days from artwork approval to delivery. Custom or overseas-sourced products can take 6–10 weeks, particularly during peak periods such as Q4 or around major sporting events.

Our rule of thumb: if your event is in six weeks’ time, you need to be placing your order today — not next week. We can accommodate rush orders in many cases, but they carry a premium and limit your product choice significantly.

5. Think About Sustainability From the Start

Sustainability is no longer optional in corporate procurement. Many of our clients now have purchasing policies that require GOTS-certified organic cotton, recycled materials, or products with verified carbon offsetting. We can source and verify all of these.

But sustainability isn’t just about materials. A product that lasts five years generates a fraction of the environmental impact of a cheap version that breaks in five weeks. Invest in quality and your brand — and the planet — benefits.

Quick tip

Ask your supplier for their ISO 14001 or B Corp credentials. Responsible suppliers will have these readily available. If they can’t provide them, that tells you something important.

6. Brief Your Supplier Properly

A good brief saves time, money, and frustration. When you approach a merchandise supplier, have the following ready:

  • Your brand guidelines or logo files (vector format preferred — .AI, .EPS, or .SVG)
  • The quantity you need — even a rough estimate
  • Your budget per unit, not just your total budget
  • Your event date and required delivery date
  • Any sustainability, ethical sourcing, or compliance requirements
  • Whether you need fulfilment, drop-shipping, or simple pallet delivery

The more clearly you brief us, the faster we can put a relevant proposal in front of you. We work with brands of every size — from 50-unit local event packs to 50,000-unit global campaign rollouts.
